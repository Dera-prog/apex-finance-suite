The brief

Turn Apex from a convincing surface into a product people can trust with real movement of money — without losing the calm, editorial feel of the brand.

Problem

Money actions were local

Deposit, withdraw, send, plans and trades used to update the browser only. A refresh, a second device or a curious user could disagree with the truth. The engineering is now server-first; the interface has to show that honestly.

Design goal

Every naira, accounted for

Each write shows three things: what will happen, that it is happening, and the server's receipt. Refusals arrive in the server's own words. Nothing is "done" until the ledger says so.

Constraint

Calm under failure

Networks fail, OTPs expire, balances run out. The design treats those as normal states, not edge cases — inline, recoverable, and never a dead end.

Design principles

Six rules that resolved most of the debates in this kit.

01

The server is the narrator

Balances, references and refusals come from the ledger. The UI may preview, but never prose over the server's story.

02

Receipt before celebration

Success is a receipt with a reference you can copy, not confetti. Money is serious; the interface should behave like it.

03

One decision per screen

Amount, then destination, then confirm. Sheets stack one level deep and always have a way back that keeps the draft.

04

Say what changed

After a write, name the new balance and move it in front of the user. Silent success reads like a bug.

05

Fail in place

Errors belong next to the field or action that caused them, with a retry that keeps the entered amount.

06

Dark is a place, not a filter

Deep royal blue, adjusted surfaces and contrast — applied before paint, respected on both apps, never leaking into the marketing site.

Who this is for

Two working personas, drawn from the product's actual demo accounts and flows.

Persona A

Chidera, 27 — first serious saver

Designs for a living in Lagos, paid monthly, saves toward rent and a laptop. Uses the phone app daily, checks balances on the bus. Wants: certainty that a transfer left, a savings plan she won't dip into, and a receipt she can screenshot.

  • One-handed use on a moving bus
  • Spotty data — offline must never invent a balance
  • Trusts numbers, not adjectives
Persona B

Tunde, 41 — cautious investor

Works in operations, holds a few NG and US shares. Uses desktop at work, mobile at night. Wants: cost visible before confirming, a clean ticket, and a transaction history that reconciles with the bank.

  • Reads the review sheet before every trade
  • Symbol-first thinking; amount-second
  • Expects search, filters and exportable proof

How we will know it works

Measurable outcomes the flows and backlog are pointed at.

Task success

≥ 95%

Deposit and transfer completed without a support message, on first attempt.

Time to proof

< 60 s

From opening the app to a receipt with a reference, for a returning user.

Error recovery

≥ 80%

Users who hit a server refusal and retry successfully in the same session.

False success

0

Cases where the UI claims success but the ledger shows no entry.

Scope note. This kit documents the shipped product and proposed optimisations. Sandbox payment and SMS keys are not configured yet, so the prototype simulates card funding and OTP delivery; the real API already enforces every balance rule shown here.
